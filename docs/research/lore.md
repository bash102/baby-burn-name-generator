# Burning Man / Regional Burn Lore: Raw Material for the Playa Name Kiosk

Raw facts and bits, not finished jokes. Content rules applied: no drug references, nothing political, sexual content kept PG-13 and marked **[PG-13]**, no personal or tragic material (deaths are left out on purpose).

Confidence tags: **[official]** means a burningman.org or Journal source. **[press]** means news coverage. **[anecdotal]** means a forum post, blog, or listicle.

## 1. Playa Names: Conventions and Real Examples

### How names get given
1. In the 2025 Burning Man Census, 63.4% of respondents said a fellow participant gave them their playa name and 23.1% said they chose it themselves. **[official]**
2. 51.0% of Census respondents used a playa name at that year's event. **[official]**
3. The common etiquette is that you don't name yourself; someone else gives you the name. (BuzzFeed: "Playa names must be given to you by others.") **[anecdotal]**
4. Names usually come from something that happens in the moment, like what you're wearing or something you did. "It's all based off of the event." **[anecdotal]**
5. Some people get a name within hours, and some never get one. **[anecdotal]**
6. Names can change from year to year. People collect several and use whichever fits that day. **[official, Journal]**
7. Departments that use radios (Rangers, Census, and others) require playa names or handles. **[academic]**
8. Real playa event: "Playa Name Ceremony – Anoint Thy Name." The nameless are told to find a veteran of ten burns, who will "divine a name that suits you." It's "never too late to have a name honored." **[official, Playa Events]**
9. Another real playa event was a "Playa Name Change Booth," which implies people show up unhappy with the name they were given. **[official, Playa Events]**

### Real names and origin stories
10. **Burping Man**: given by his theme camp because he can belch loudly while talking in full paragraphs. **[academic]**
11. **Stuck Truck**: a long-time burner's community-given name (the backstory writes itself). **[anecdotal]**
12. **Wee Heavy**: named after a beer from AleSmith Brewing. **[academic]**
13. **Gadget / Gadget Fairy**: given by the Census team. **[academic]**
14. **The Countess**: the Census manager chose it because, like the Count on Sesame Street, she counts Burners. Her other names were **Bunny** (she wore bunny ears that day), **Chameleon** (nobody recognized her in costume), and **Ginger Snap** (her temper). **[academic]**
15. The Census team adopted math names: **Equator, Variance, Random**. **[academic]**
16. **Blue Cross**: the Exodus Manager's name. **[academic]**
17. **Chicken John**: friends said he looked like a chicken when he got angry. **[academic]**
18. **Atlas**: given on Burn night to a man pushing a giant metal globe that people climbed. **[official, Journal]**
19. **Presence**: given to a friend who silently joined the group and was always reminding people to stay present. **[official, Journal]**
20. Rejected candidates in that same story: **Apollo** ("didn't commit") and **Hephaestus** ("too obscure"). Bureaucracy applies even to naming. **[official, Journal]**
21. More real names from the Journal: **Laser Wolf, Huggles, Gummi Bear, Angel Eyes, Puck, Artichoke, Rufio, Songbird, Peter Pan, Socrates, Nibs, Gonzo, Beating Heart**. **[official, Journal]**
22. Founder handles: **Danger Ranger** (Michael Mikel, who founded the Black Rock Rangers in 1992) and **Crimson Rose** (who started the Fire Conclave). **[official/press]**
23. Ranger ranks at the Alchemy regional: **Alpha** (novice Ranger), **Dirt** (experienced), **Khaki** (stationed at HQ). **[regional glossary]**
24. Alchemy's Public Works volunteers are all called **"Timmy,"** after a joke about interchangeable helpers. **[regional glossary]**

### Naming patterns seen across examples (for the generator)
- Mishap + object ("Stuck Truck")
- Bodily talent ("Burping Man")
- Beverage or food ("Wee Heavy," "Artichoke," "Gummi Bear," "Ginger Snap")
- Costume item ("Bunny")
- Mythic or grandiose ("Atlas," "Apollo," "Socrates")
- Adjective + animal ("Laser Wolf")
- Cute diminutive ("Huggles")
- Job-pun ("The Countess")

## 2. Insider Lore and Jargon

### Arrival rituals
25. Greeters hug arrivals and say **"Welcome Home!"** **[official]**
26. First-timers ("virgins") ring a bell or hit a gong, roll in the dust, make "dust angels," and shout that they are no longer a virgin, sometimes three times. **[official/anecdotal]**
27. Veterans who claim they aren't first-timers skip the bell, and some people lie to get out of it. **[anecdotal]**
28. The speed limit is **10 mph on Gate Road** and **5 mph inside Black Rock City**. That applies to e-bikes and art cars too. **[official]**

### Core vocabulary
29. **MOOP** (Matter Out Of Place) covers any trash, plus anything not native to the desert: orange peels, water spills, a single sequin. **[official/regional]**
30. **POOP** (People Out Of Place) is a lost human who needs returning to their camp. **[regional glossary]**
31. **Default World** means everything outside Black Rock City. **[official]**
32. **Decomp** (decompression) is the post-burn letdown after you return to the Default World. **[regional]**
33. **Sparkle Pony** is a derogatory term for someone who shows up with no food or water and "suitcases full of costumes," and does no work. **[regional]**
34. **Darkwad** is someone walking the playa at night with no lights, and a hazard. **[regional]**
35. **Ground Score** is a useful thing someone dropped that you keep (valuables should go to Lost & Found). **[regional]**
36. **Playa Magic** is the moment you get exactly what you needed, right when you needed it. **[anecdotal]**
37. **Burner** is a participant, whether at the event or living the principles year-round. **[anecdotal]**
38. **Trash Fence** is the perimeter fence, first used in 1996 to catch blowing debris. **[anecdotal]**
39. **Art car / Mutant Vehicle**: these must be registered with the **DMV**, the Department of Mutant Vehicles. **[official/regional]**
40. **Exodus** is leaving at the end of the week. Expect 3 to 5 hours (2007 guidance), 6 to 9 hours at recent peaks, and 9+ hours officially in 2022. **[official/press]**
41. **"Burning Man was better last year"** is a veteran adage said so often it became a joke. It's printed on a button that gets handed out every year. **[press]**
42. **Lamplighters** are volunteers who light lanterns along the roads every night. **[regional/official]**
43. **Plus/Delta** is the post-event "what worked, what didn't" meeting. **[regional]**

### The Ten Principles (written by Larry Harvey in 2004) **[official]**
44. Radical Inclusion, Gifting, Decommodification, Radical Self-reliance, Radical Self-expression, Communal Effort, Civic Responsibility, Leaving No Trace, Participation, Immediacy.
45. Decommodification means nothing is for sale. Historically, the only things you could buy were **ice (Arctica) and coffee/tea/lemonade at Center Camp Café**. Coffee was later made free, and older accounts note lemonade used to be free too. **[official/press]**
46. Arctica ice depots go by **"Ice Main," "Ice Cubed" (3:00 & G), and "Ice Nine" (9:00 & G)**. They take cards and Apple Pay. **[official]**
47. Ice was about $3 a bag (2016). Rembert Browne said "Ice is worth more than Google stocks out there." **[press]**

### Black Rock City layout **[official]**
48. Radial streets are named like a clock face (2:00 through 10:00, half-hours like 4:30). The Man sits at the center.
49. Ring streets run alphabetically outward. **Esplanade** is the innermost ring, followed by A, B, C...
50. Each year the lettered ring streets are renamed to fit the art theme. In 2015 (Carnival of Mirrors) they were **Arcade, Ballyhoo, Carny, Donniker...** ("donniker" is carny slang for a toilet). In 2023 (Animalia) they were fabulous folklore beasts, and in 2024 (Curiouser & Curiouser) they came from Alice in Wonderland.
51. **Center Camp** sits at 6:00 & Esplanade. It has the café, an open stage, and a giant message board. **[official/anecdotal]**
52. Black Rock City becomes roughly **Nevada's third-largest city** for the week, at about 70,000 people. **[press]**
53. It has its own airport, **88NV**, with two 6,000-ft dirt runways and about 500 takeoffs and landings a day at peak. Pilots must read **32 pages of airport rules**. "88" is ham-radio shorthand for "hugs & kisses." **[official/press]**
54. There's a real **Black Rock City Post Office** with its own postmark. It's run by the Cacophony crew who first appeared as armed "Disgruntled Postal Workers" delivering fake mail. **[press]**
55. The city has a newspaper. **[anecdotal]**
56. A random sample of participants is surveyed for the **Census**, and the official advice is "just say yes!" **[official]**

### The Man and the Temple
57. The first Man (June 22, 1986, Baker Beach, San Francisco) was about **8 feet tall**, built from scrap lumber by Larry Harvey and Jerry James. Some accounts add a smaller wooden dog. **[press]**
58. The event moved to the Black Rock Desert in 1990 as a Cacophony Society outing called **"Bad Day at Black Rock (Zone Trip #4)."** **[press]**
59. The Man burns on Saturday night, loudly, with fireworks. The Temple burns on Sunday, almost silently. **[press]**
60. In 1996 pranksters put a **giant neon smiley face** in the Man's head. Larry Harvey was not amused and had it removed immediately. **[press]**
61. The Man was meant to evoke Da Vinci's Vitruvian Man and was supposed to rotate, but it didn't. **[anecdotal]**
62. David Best has built about 15 Temples. **[anecdotal]**

### Thunderdome
63. Death Guild (a San Francisco goth club that opened in 1993) has run the **Thunderdome** since 1999. It's a Mad Max replica where two people hang from bungee harnesses and whack each other with **foam/Nerf bats** while the crowd climbs the dome. **[press]**
64. One famous bout: **two swinging T-Rexes** fighting with bats. **[press]**
65. Thunderdome is taking a break in 2026 and plans to return in 2027. **[press]**

### Rebar, bikes, gear
66. Burning Man says **most injuries trace back to collisions with rebar** (tent stakes). The official advice is to cover it with tennis balls or upside-down soda bottles, or "HAMMER THAT SUCKER ALL THE WAY IN!" **[official]**
67. **Yellow Bikes** are community bikes. Taking one home counts as theft. In one year only about 80% came back. **[official]**
68. Bikes left behind range from about 600 one year to about 2,000 (2014) to roughly 3,000 (2017). The Reno Bike Project and Kiwanis refurbish them and resell them to next year's burners. **[press]**
69. Reno police once blamed a spike in local bike thefts on Burning Man. The Reno Bike Project said it was just more people riding. **[press]**
70. One burner bought a returned floor-model bike at a **Reno Walmart for $55**. There's an AP photo of burners loading up in the Reno Walmart parking lot, and a CS Monitor headline reads "Burning Man 'starts' with a night in Walmart parking lot." **[press/anecdotal]**
71. Reno-Tahoe Airport has a drop-off for unwanted Burning Man bikes. **[press]**
72. The **Burner Express Bus** runs from Reno (from $159 one way) and San Francisco (from $194). **[press]**
73. Goggles are "a necessity, not a fashion statement." Steampunk goggles count as "practically necessary." **[anecdotal]**
74. Faux fur is "the playa's unofficial night wear" (per a costume retailer). The feather-and-leather look comes from the El Circo camp. **[anecdotal]**
75. Wear lights at night or you're a darkwad and cyclists will hit you. **[anecdotal]**

### Porta-potty culture
76. Black Rock City has roughly **1,700 porta-potties**, and the **Pottie Project** volunteers visit each one to promote etiquette. **[official]**
77. The official rule: "If it didn't come out of your body, it doesn't go in the potty." Baby wipes count as trash. **[official]**
78. Official Burning Man pages really use the phrases **"Potty Humor"** and **"Excremental Correctness."** **[official]**
79. One group rolled out a **red carpet and velvet ropes** so people leaving a porta-potty got applause. **[official podcast]**
80. Bathrooms are "surprisingly clean because the air is so dry." **[anecdotal]**
81. You can **bowl while waiting in the porta-potty line**. **[anecdotal]**
82. Organizers have asked people to stop pooping in the "deep playa." **[press]**
83. A **Potty List** email forum existed (2001) to discuss toilet issues. **[official]**

## 3. Survival Tips, Folk Remedies, and the Detergent Question

### What playa dust actually is
84. It's not sand. It's dried alkaline lakebed mud from **Pleistocene Lake Lahontan**, which 13,000 years ago covered 8,665 sq mi of northwest Nevada. **[official]**
85. The dust is alkaline and salty at the same time. It's corrosive enough to **rust metal**. **[official/anecdotal]**
86. It grays your hair. Sweat rubs it off, "so skipping showers feels fine." **[anecdotal]**

### Playa foot
87. Burning Man officially calls playa foot "a malady unique to the Black Rock Desert... in essence, a chemical burn" caused by alkali dust. **[official]**
88. The folk remedy is a **white vinegar** rinse or soak (acid neutralizes base), then lotion or oil. Prevention means socks and closed shoes. **[official/anecdotal]**
89. A 2003 study found weak acids like vinegar neutralize alkali burns more than 15 minutes faster than water alone. That study was about household alkalis, not playa. **[press]**
90. Caveats: one poster said the vinegar was "very soothing, but as soon as the feet were dry, the playa-foot was as bad as ever." Vinegar on cracked feet burns. **[anecdotal]**
91. Burning Man's own page says cleaning goes easier with a mildly acidic solution such as **vinegar or carbonated water**. **[official]**

### THE DETERGENT ANSWER: Calgon
92. The brand burners most consistently say actually gets playa dust out is **Calgon** (the water softener / laundry booster), used together with ordinary laundry soap. **[anecdotal, multiple sources]**
93. Recipe from a popular playa guide (16 oz batch): **8 oz water, 6 oz Calgon, 2 oz laundry soap.** "The mixture that seems to work best is the combination of water, Calgon, and laundry soap." **[anecdotal]**
94. The claimed reason: vinegar only partly neutralizes the alkali, and Calgon's water-softening and chelating action binds the minerals so the dust lets go. **[anecdotal]**
95. ePlaya advice is a cold-water rinse or soak with vinegar, plus "Calgon water softener, found in the laundry aisle." **[anecdotal]**
96. An eplaya user recommends blowing gear out with a compressed-air blow gun or shop vac first. **[anecdotal]**
97. Others are firmly in the "machine wash, regular detergent, whatever" camp. **[anecdotal]**
98. Knitters warn: don't bring good wool. **[anecdotal]**
99. **The laundromat incident (Sept 2026)**: burners dropped a huge load at Bali Laundry, a brand-new laundromat in SF's Cole Valley, while it was unattended. A washer jammed, and the next morning the owners found fine dust coating machines and surfaces all over the shop and caked inside dryer drums. A couple of dryers stayed out of service until they could be professionally deep-cleaned. The customers paid for the cleanup. Commenters' advice: vinegar plus Calgon, and shake everything out outside first. **[press]**

### Hydration, food, drink
100. Water alone isn't enough when you're sweating and dancing, so pack electrolyte packets. **[anecdotal]**
101. Canned **V8** is a veteran's salty rehydration trick. **[anecdotal]**
102. Strangers drive by on art cars handing out alcohol and **snow cones**. **[anecdotal]**
103. Bacon is a running joke: "a gateway meat for vegetarians." People hand out **bacon at midnight**, and bartenders **blow-torch bacon** for Bloody Marys. **[official Journal/press]**
104. Grilled cheese is a recurring camp gift, sometimes served from a **diner art car**, and the line is "part of the experience." **[press]**
105. One gifter fills a garbage bag with ice and hands out cold watermelon in deep playa. **[anecdotal]**
106. Coverage was thin on Tecate, warm beer, pickles, and Costco runs. They're widely known lore, but no good source turned up, so treat them as unverified.

### Dust storms / whiteouts
107. In a whiteout, don't drive. If you can't see landmarks, stay put. **[official]**
108. A veteran reported an **8-hour dust storm with 70 mph winds**. **[anecdotal]**
109. Everfest's creative director said the most romantic moment he ever had at Burning Man was clutching his girlfriend's hand while crossing the playa in a total whiteout. **[anecdotal]**
110. "Ski goggles work. You might already have them." A painter's mask beats a fashionable bandana. **[anecdotal]**

### Mud year (2023)
111. Rain turned the playa into ankle-deep mud, gates closed, and about 73,000 people were told to shelter in place and conserve food, water, and fuel. **[press]**
112. **Diplo and Chris Rock walked out about 5 to 6 miles through the mud**, and a fan named Tony gave them a ride. Diplo tweeted it, then DJed a show in DC that night. **[press]**
113. Diplo said Rock "was really bizarrely scared" and predicted it would become a bit in Rock's next special. **[press]**
114. Exodus after the mud took about 7 hours officially. One Redditor reported 13 hours. **[press]**

## 4. Ridiculous Things People Have Done in the Desert

### Art cars / mutant vehicles
115. **El Pulpo Mecánico**: Duane Flatmo's 26-ft fire-breathing mechanical octopus, built on a donated 1973 Ford F-250 from salvage-yard scrap. He sold it to someone in Texas and built a bigger one, **El Pulpo Magnifico**. **[press]**
116. **Mayan Warrior**: a Mexico City crew's laser-and-sound art car, first on playa around 2011–2012. It burned in a road fire in 2023, and its successor, the "Galaxyer" (a "futuristic Mayan spaceship"), cost nearly $3M. **[press]**
117. A **giant rubber duck** built on top of a bus, with a dance floor and lasers. There's also a flaming duck. **[anecdotal]**
118. **Ripper the Shark**: a 40-ft silver shark car (1996). There's also Tom Kennedy's **Great White Whale**, with a moving tail and a fire-spewing blowhole. **[anecdotal/press]**
119. **Space Wench**, a ship art car, carried up to 60 people and **hosted three weddings** over 13 runs. **[anecdotal]**
120. A "fully motile UFO-like couch with built-in speakers" (Gizmodo), and a **motorized couch (broken down)** in 2000 photos. **[press]**
121. Seen cruising the playa: tiny cupcake cars, a giant armadillo, a pink rabbit, a dino-mobile, a rainbow unicorn with a viewing deck, a steampunk zeppelin with a crystal chandelier, and a giant scorpion car that was later listed on eBay. **[press/anecdotal]**
122. A **Spam-themed vehicle** (the author wished it were a Spam jacuzzi). **[anecdotal]**
123. An **airplane art car** ("a really big party bus for deep thinkers," per Vice). **[press]**
124. Burning Man has a literal DMV. Mutant vehicles must be registered, and an unregistered one can be ejected. **[official]**

### Art installations
125. **Big Rig Jig (2007)**: two 18-wheel oil tankers welded into a 42-ft sculpture that looks like the trucks are somersaulting. **[press]**
126. **Space Whale**: a giant mother humpback diving from the sky to her calf, built by Reno artists, with a whole documentary about it. **[press]**
127. A giant **Facebook "Like" thumb** you could worship. **[anecdotal]**
128. A spinning monkey sculpture that is "both awesome and creepy." **[anecdotal]**
129. A giant **BORING** sign that people dance around. **[anecdotal]**
130. A giant ball pit for adults. **[anecdotal]**

### Camps and stunts
137. **B.A.R.K. (Bolt Action Rifle Klub)** was the very first "theme camp," in 1990. **[press]**
138. **Hug Deli** offers a menu of hug varieties. **[press]**

### Weddings
150. Dozens of weddings happen each year, usually at sunrise or sunset. **[official/anecdotal]**
151. Forget your Nevada marriage license and you drive to Reno or Lovelock to get one, then pay **$20 per person to get back in**. **[anecdotal]**
152. A chapel on stilts was a popular wedding spot until it was burned. **[anecdotal]**
153. Couples have married at the Temple at sunrise. Many have "playa weddings" that aren't legally binding. There's an official email address for weddings. **[official/press]**
154. There's an art car church, the **Church of the Open Mind**, that hosts weddings. **[anecdotal]**

### Orgy Dome [PG-13, keep it light]
155. It's a long-running camp (since 2003 or 2004, sources disagree) famous mostly for its **very strict consent rules**: a mandatory consent talk from a "Dome Greeter" at the door, plus a list of rules inside. It isn't officially affiliated with Burning Man. **[press]**
156. In 2025 high winds destroyed the dome. Headlines reported "one casualty: the Orgy Dome," and it ran a GoFundMe for $30,000 to rebuild. **[press]**
157. A separate camp, the Bureau of Erotic Discourse, tapes consent posters inside porta-potties. **[press]** [PG-13]

160. **Burning Japan** burns a **phoenix**. **[press]**
